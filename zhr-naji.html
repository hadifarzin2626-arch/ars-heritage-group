INTELLIGENT SAFETY & RISK AWARENESS

See The Risk Before It Becomes The Emergency.

The future of protection is not only about reacting to danger.

It is about recognizing the signs that may appear before danger becomes an emergency.

The earlier a meaningful risk is recognized, the more time people may have to respond.

From Detection To Understanding.

A sensor can detect movement.

A camera can see an object.

A vehicle can report its speed.

But none of these signals alone can fully explain what is happening.

Intelligent safety begins when multiple sources of information can be evaluated together.

ZHR NAJI is designed to move toward a future in which relevant information is not simply collected, but interpreted within context.

The goal is not to collect more data. The goal is to understand what matters.

THE RISK AWARENESS MODEL

Five Steps From Signal To Safety.

The long-term vision of ZHR NAJI is based on a continuous safety cycle.

01

OBSERVE

Monitor relevant information from supported sensors, cameras, vehicle systems and authorized user inputs.

02

UNDERSTAND

Interpret available information and identify meaningful relationships between multiple signals.

03

ASSESS

Evaluate the potential significance of an event based on context, available evidence and confidence.

04

WARN

Communicate meaningful information when attention or action may be required.

05

SUPPORT

Help the person take an appropriate next step through guidance, assistance or authorized communication.

CONTEXTUAL INTELLIGENCE

The Same Signal Can Mean Different Things.

A sudden movement may be harmless.

Or it may be a warning sign.

A vehicle stopped on the roadside may be normal.

Or it may indicate a problem.

A passenger may be sitting quietly.

Or they may need assistance.

A single signal rarely tells the entire story.

Context changes meaning.

ZHR NAJI is therefore designed around the principle that safety decisions should consider available context rather than relying blindly on one isolated signal.

MULTI-SIGNAL ANALYSIS

When Signals Work Together.

A more complete understanding can emerge when multiple relevant sources of information are evaluated together.

CAMERA + SENSOR

Visual information combined with sensor data may provide greater awareness of the surrounding environment.

VEHICLE + ENVIRONMENT

Vehicle conditions can be evaluated alongside environmental information to better understand the situation.

TIME + LOCATION

Where appropriate and authorized, contextual information may help distinguish between normal and unusual conditions.

HUMAN + MACHINE

The most meaningful safety decisions may emerge when intelligent systems support human judgment rather than attempting to replace it.

RISK PRIORITIZATION

Not Every Alert Is An Emergency.

One of the most important responsibilities of an intelligent safety system is knowing when to speak and when not to.

01

AWARENESS

A situation that may be useful for the user to know about, without requiring immediate action.

02

ATTENTION

A condition that may require the user's attention or a change in awareness.

03

WARNING

A potentially meaningful risk where the user may need to respond promptly.

04

CRITICAL

A high-priority situation where immediate attention or assistance may be required.

RESPONSIBLE ALERTING

Too Many Warnings Can Become A Safety Risk.

An intelligent safety system must understand that unnecessary alerts can create fatigue, distraction and eventually cause people to ignore important warnings.

For this reason, ZHR NAJI is designed around the principle of meaningful alerting.

The system should consider relevance, confidence and context before escalating an event.

When uncertainty is high, the system should communicate that uncertainty rather than presenting assumptions as facts.

A warning should be important enough to deserve attention.

PREDICTIVE SAFETY

From Reactive Protection To Preventive Awareness.

The long-term vision of ZHR NAJI is to move progressively from reacting to events toward recognizing patterns that may indicate elevated risk.

This may involve identifying changes in driving conditions, unusual vehicle behavior, repeated warning patterns or other signals that could indicate a developing situation.

The system may then provide earlier awareness or suggest that the user pay attention to a specific condition.

Predictive capabilities must always be treated carefully.

They should never be presented as certainty when the underlying evidence is uncertain.

Prediction is assistance. It is never a guarantee.

HUMAN-CENTERED DECISION MAKING

Intelligence Should Support Human Judgment.

ZHR NAJI is designed to assist people, not to remove their ability to make decisions.

When a situation is uncertain, the system should provide information that helps the person understand what may be happening.

The user should remain aware of the system's limitations.

Where appropriate, the user should be able to acknowledge, dismiss or respond to alerts.

The system should never create the illusion that an automated decision is always correct.

The machine can observe. The machine can analyze. The machine can warn. But human life remains at the center of the decision.

ROSE × INTELLIGENT SAFETY

When Intelligence Needs A Voice.

Complex safety information is not always easy to understand.

This is where ROSE can become an important part of the experience.

Instead of presenting raw technical information, ROSE can help communicate the meaning of an event in a clearer and more human-centered way.

For example, rather than simply reporting a technical sensor event, the system could communicate the practical significance of the situation in a way that is easier for the user to understand.

The exact communication method will depend on the validated capabilities of the system.

ZHR NAJI understands the risk. ROSE helps the human understand the message.

CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

A Protection System That Learns From Experience.

The world is too complex for any system to be considered complete on its first day.

ZHR NAJI is designed as an evolving platform.

As validated engineering knowledge grows, new capabilities can be introduced through software improvements, updated models and future hardware modules.

Where appropriate, system performance can be evaluated through testing, real-world validation and carefully controlled feedback.

The objective is not to create a system that claims to know everything.

The objective is to build a system that becomes better because we continue to learn.

HONESTY ABOUT LIMITATIONS

Intelligence Must Know Its Limits.

No artificial intelligence system is perfect.

No sensor is perfect.

No camera is perfect.

No prediction is perfect.

And no technology can guarantee that an accident or emergency will never happen.

Responsible intelligent safety requires recognizing these limitations.

ZHR NAJI must therefore be designed to communicate uncertainty, handle failure responsibly and avoid creating false confidence.

The safest intelligent system is not the one that claims to know everything. It is the one that knows when it may be wrong.

EMERGENCY ASSISTANCE & CRISIS RESPONSE

When Every Second Matters.

The purpose of protection is not only to help prevent danger.

It is also to be prepared for the moments when danger becomes real.

When prevention is no longer enough, the next responsibility is to help people respond.

Protection Does Not End When An Emergency Begins.

A serious event can change everything in seconds.

A collision.

A sudden medical situation.

A dangerous environmental condition.

A person who becomes unable to respond.

A child who needs immediate assistance.

An elderly person who is suddenly alone.

In these moments, confusion can become another danger.

ZHR NAJI is designed to provide an additional layer of intelligent support that can help recognize critical situations, communicate meaningful information and support the next appropriate step.

The system cannot guarantee the outcome. But it can be designed to help people reach the right help faster.

THE EMERGENCY RESPONSE CYCLE

Recognize. Confirm. Alert. Connect. Support.

The emergency response philosophy of ZHR NAJI is built around a structured sequence designed to reduce confusion during critical moments.

01

RECOGNIZE

Identify signals that may indicate a serious event or emergency condition.

This may include supported vehicle information, sensor data, camera information or other relevant system inputs.

02

CONFIRM

Where technically possible and appropriate, the system should attempt to determine whether the detected condition is meaningful and avoid unnecessary escalation.

When confirmation is not possible, uncertainty should be communicated honestly.

03

ALERT

Provide clear and immediate information to the people inside the vehicle when their attention may be required.

04

CONNECT

Where authorized and supported, help establish communication with trusted contacts or appropriate assistance channels.

05

SUPPORT

Continue providing appropriate information and assistance while the situation is being managed.

ACCIDENT AWARENESS

When Something Goes Wrong.

Recognizing A Potential Collision Or Serious Event.

A major accident may create a situation in which the people inside the vehicle are unable to respond normally.

ZHR NAJI is designed to explore the use of available vehicle and environmental signals to identify conditions that may indicate a serious incident.

Depending on the validated hardware and software capabilities, relevant signals may include vehicle data, impact-related information, sensor states or other supported indicators.

The system should not assume that every unusual event is an accident.

It should evaluate available information carefully and communicate uncertainty when the evidence is insufficient.

The goal is not to guess. The goal is to recognize responsibly.

HUMAN CONFIRMATION

When Possible, Ask The Person First.

Not Every Emergency Signal Means The Same Thing.

When a potentially serious event is detected and the person is able to respond, the system may provide an opportunity for confirmation.

ROSE can help communicate a clear question and guide the user through the next available options.

If the user confirms that assistance is needed, the system can proceed according to the authorized emergency workflow.

If the user confirms that everything is fine, unnecessary escalation may be avoided when appropriate.

If there is no response, the system may follow the predefined emergency policy and available authorization settings.

The human remains at the center whenever the human is able to respond.

WHEN THERE IS NO RESPONSE

Silence Can Also Be A Signal.

One of the most difficult situations occurs when a serious event may have happened and the person cannot respond.

In such situations, the system must follow carefully defined rules based on available evidence, user authorization and applicable regulations.

ZHR NAJI may be designed to escalate the situation according to predefined priorities.

This could include notifying authorized family members or trusted contacts.

It could include communicating relevant information through supported emergency channels.

Or it may simply continue providing local assistance and guidance when external communication is unavailable.

The system should never pretend to know more than it knows. But it should also never ignore a credible sign of danger.

FAMILY & TRUSTED CONTACTS

When Someone You Love May Need Help.

In a serious situation, families often need information as much as they need reassurance.

Where authorized by the user, ZHR NAJI may support communication with designated family members or trusted contacts.

The information shared should be relevant to the situation and limited according to privacy and authorization requirements.

The goal is not to create constant surveillance.

The goal is to help trusted people become aware when their support may genuinely matter.

The right information. To the right people. At the right time.

EMERGENCY COMMUNICATION

Connecting People With The Help They Need.

ROSE × EMERGENCY ASSISTANCE

A Calm Voice In An Uncertain Moment.

ROSE is designed to provide a calmer and more understandable interface during these moments.

It can explain what may happen next.

In a crisis, clarity can be a form of protection.

ACCESSIBLE EMERGENCY SUPPORT

A person who cannot hear should be able to receive important information through visual or tactile channels where available.

A person with a disability should have access to interfaces designed around their needs whenever technically possible.

Children and elderly people may require simpler communication and additional support.

The emergency message must find the person, not simply be sent into the system.

Signal can disappear.

A vehicle may travel through an area with limited coverage.

For this reason, ZHR NAJI should be designed with graceful degradation in mind.

When external communication is unavailable, local safety functions should continue operating where technically possible.

The system should clearly communicate when an external connection cannot be established.

A system should never create the illusion that help has been contacted when it has not.

Potentially sensitive events.
